# Largest home, private dock and first steps — 11 Bayview Cir and 5001 Arrowhead Pt (10 Nov 2026)

Desk study from public records; not a survey, design or permit decision. Full version with figures: [`Bayview-Arrowhead-BigHome-Dock-DD-and-Emails_10-Nov-26_Claude-Opus-5.5.pdf`](../../Bayview-Arrowhead-BigHome-Dock-DD-and-Emails_10-Nov-26_Claude-Opus-5.5.pdf).

## Verdicts

**A · 11 Bayview Cir.** Big home: yes. HOA sewer means no drip field, so only the set-backs, the wedge shape and Zone A limit the house: up to a 116×52 ft footprint, ≈9,424 sf heated on two storeys in theory. Private dock: yes, up to 1,200 sf in a basin about 98 ft across, shared with two neighbours' boathouses. Main unknown: water depth. TWDB never sounded the basin, so sound it before you buy. Also check the HOA sewer plant, which settled a TCEQ order for 2023–24 effluent violations.

**B · 5001 Arrowhead Pt.** Big home: yes, but the septic drip field sets the limit: ≈8,912–9,904 sf heated in theory. That is far above the 2,200–3,500 sf of Rustling Oaks' most valuable homes. Private dock: yes, up to 918 sf. Water is 6 ft deep at full pool and 3 ft today, 30 ft out from the bank (TWDB survey). Low holding cost ($144/yr dues), but the CC&Rs require the outside walls and roof to be finished within 120 days of starting, and they ban private wells.

## A · 11 Bayview Cir

- (+) HOA sewer: no septic field, so the most room for a house, pool and yard
- (+) Cheapest way into White Rock: the CAD values Lot 11 at $85,197 of land, about half the median for the 12 vacant White Rock lots ($166k)
- (+) Two neighbours built 977- and 1,024-sf boathouses on the same water in 2023, so TRWD has approved boathouses here
- (+) Gated, 16 of 28 lots built, median home value about $1.2M (CAD), so a large home fits the street
- (+) Steel bulkhead already in place (CAD: 170 ft, 2015)
- (+) Asking price equals the CAD value; listed on and off since 2022, so there is room to negotiate
- (−) Basin depth unknown: the TWDB 2018 soundings stop about 200 ft away, where the bottom is 306.3 ft (8.7 ft deep at full pool, 5.9 ft today)
- (−) The basin is 60–110 ft across at full pool, so one third of its width limits how far a dock can reach (about 26 ft here)
- (−) The lot is a wedge that narrows to the water; 13% of it (the shore strip) is FEMA Zone A
- (−) HOA dues $3,530/yr while you hold the lot (MLS figure, sewer included)
- (−) White Rock's CC&Rs are not online. Height limits, set-backs from the water, boathouse rules and build-time limits are all unknown
- (!) The HOA's sewer plant (TCEQ permit WQ0014821002, 8,000 gal/day for 28 lots) broke its effluent limits for at least nine months in 2023–24. The TCEQ agreed order (Texas Register notice, 19 Dec 2025) deferred $18,150 of a $21,750 penalty for financial inability to pay. Ask about special assessments, plant upgrades and spare capacity before you commit
- (!) The listing says “deep water”. That describes the main lake, not the basin in front of the lot
- (!) The Teel team lists this lot and also advertises as buyer's agents. Use your own buyer's agent or get the intermediary terms in writing

Before you commit:

1. Before any offer, sound the basin on site: depth at 10, 20, 30 ft out, at full pool and today (a marked pole from a kayak works).
2. Get the CC&Rs, the HOA budget and the sewer plant's status in writing (HOA e-mail).
3. Ask TRWD whether a 1,200-sf boathouse fits beside the two existing ones, and whether dredging is allowed if the basin is shallow.
4. Offer ≈$80–85k with a 21–30-day option. Make it subject to TRWD's dock answer and the HOA's written sewer-tap confirmation.
5. Survey in the option period: corners, 315/320-ft lines, Zone A line, bulkhead position.

## B · 5001 Arrowhead Pt

- (+) Lowest carrying cost: $144/yr POA dues (recorded CC&Rs) and about $970–1,180/yr tax today
- (+) Phase II was platted in 1990, so TRWD's 1-acre septic rule (lots split after 8 Apr 2011) does not apply; the legal 0.500 ac meets TCEQ's ½-acre minimum
- (+) Real survey depth under the dock: 6.1 ft at full pool, 3.2 ft today; a 49-ft reach gives 7.6 / 4.8 ft
- (+) Flat, high ground: 99.8% above 320 ft, only 2.5% in Zone A
- (+) Short, permissive CC&Rs: 1,600 sf minimum, 5-ft side and rear set-backs, no size or height cap
- (+) TRWD's Richland-Chambers office is in Streetman, 140 FM 416
- (−) Asking $99,900 is 1.45× the 2026 CAD value ($68,849), which itself rose 45% in one year
- (−) The tax is capped now (taxable $56,796, the 20% circuit breaker). The cap ends for a buyer: ≈$1,540/yr at a $90k value
- (−) Over-building risk: Rustling Oaks' most valuable homes are 2,200–3,500 sf (CAD up to $844k)
- (−) The drip field limits the house; a pool would compete with it for space
- (−) Developer Kodiak TJ LLC still owns 54 vacant lots nearby, which may hold resale prices down
- (!) CC&R ¶H: outside walls painted and roof finished within 120 days of starting. That is tight for a large custom home; agree how the ACC counts the 120 days before you pour the slab
- (!) CC&R ¶J: owners may not drill water wells. ¶G: metal roofs need ACC approval
- (!) The CAD GIS lot line runs 8–21 ft back from the full-pool water's edge, near the 320-ft contour. The survey must show where the plat line is, and TRWD must agree on the shoreline length
- (!) The seller owns the house next door. Check that none of 5003's septic, drip lines, water line, fence or irrigation sits on Lot 33
- (!) POA roads take 6-yard concrete loads only (50,000 lb), so a big slab means more truck trips and short-load fees

Before you commit:

1. Ask the POA/ACC how they apply the 120-day rule to a large home, and confirm the $144 vs $644 fee ($644 may be dues plus the $500 road fee).
2. Ask TRWD how it measures the shoreline here and whether a 30–50-ft walkway is acceptable.
3. Offer ≈$85–90k with a 21–30-day option, subject to a septic site evaluation for 6 bedrooms and the survey.
4. In the option period, get the survey (plat line vs 315/320 ft, encroachments from 5003) and the septic evaluation.
5. Walk away if the drip field will not fit your bedroom count, or the survey shows 5003's systems on the lot.

## The area

- (+) Richland-Chambers is Texas's third-largest inland lake: 43,874 ac at full pool, 25.6 ft mean and 76.8 ft maximum depth (TWDB 2018)
- (+) Both house pads sit near 327–329 ft, 9–11 ft above TRWD's 318-ft 100-year lake level, so no fill is needed
- (+) TRWD's 2026 rules are published and cheap to use: dock permit $300, septic permit $400, 10 + 30 business days review
- (+) Navarro County grew 8.6% from 2020 to 2025
- (−) The lake was below 312 ft on 27% of days since 2000; the 2006 low (303.6 ft) dried both coves. Expect seasons when a lift sits in mud
- (−) Expansive clay (shrink-swell LEP ≈9%): plan an engineered post-tensioned slab or piers; for a big house budget $60–120k for foundation and pad
- (−) FEMA NRI rates tornado and wind risk relatively high and hail moderate: wind/hail deductibles and the roof choice matter
- (−) Property tax runs 1.57% (A) to 1.71% (B) of value: a $2M home ≈$31k/yr, a $1.4M home ≈$24k/yr before any homestead exemption
- (!) Order of approvals: HOA/ACC → TRWD (dock, walls; septic for B) → county building permit (needs the OSSF permit number and a site plan) → three third-party inspections → 911 address
- (!) Over-improvement: lenders and appraisers compare with nearby sales. A very large house can appraise below its cost, which matters if you need a construction loan
- (!) Lake water belongs to TRWD: irrigation from the lake and any work below 320 ft need a TRWD permit
